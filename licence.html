Attribution (BY)

Give clear and prominent credit: name the authors (Ammon Salter, Warwick Business School, with Stefano Baruffaldi, Politecnico di Milano, and Federico Bignone, Warwick Business School), include the citation below, state the licence, and indicate any changes you made. The credit is the citation. Citations are how the development team gains credit for this work, so if you use the game in teaching, a syllabus, a talk or a publication, cite it as you would any other source.

NonCommercial (NC) — free educational and research use

No permission or payment is needed where the primary purpose is teaching, learning or research. You do not need to ask; we do like hearing how it went. Free uses include:

  • • Undergraduate and postgraduate degree programmes
  • • In-class sessions, seminars, tutorials and assessment built on the game
  • • MOOCs and open online courses
  • • Summer schools, pre-sessional and outreach programmes, including schools and colleges
  • • Student competitions, societies, enterprise programmes and hackathons
  • • Dissertations and student projects that use or study the game
  • • Teacher training and educator development
  • • Self-directed learning by individuals for personal or professional development

Research use is free in the same way, and welcome:

  • • Studies of entrepreneurial learning, decision-making and simulation-based teaching that use the game
  • • Collecting journey reports or play data from your own students as research data, under your institution's ethics approval
  • • Adapting the game as a research instrument — controlled scenarios, treatment and control versions, added measures
  • • Replication, methods teaching and doctoral training

NonCommercial (NC) — what needs a separate licence

Any context where the simulation helps generate fees or revenue:

  • • Executive education charged to participants or their employers
  • • Corporate training and staff development programmes
  • • Consulting engagements that use the simulation with clients
  • • Paid workshops, bootcamps and accelerator programmes with fees
  • • Reselling, sublicensing, or bundling the simulation into paid products or services

The test is whether the simulation is part of what is being sold. Degree teaching at a fee-charging university is free educational use: students pay tuition for the degree, and the game is one of many things used to teach it. It is different when a course is sold and the simulation is included in it. An open-enrolment executive course, a company programme, a consultant's workshop: if the fee covers the session where the game is played, that needs a licence. If in doubt, ask.

If your case sits between the two lists, ask: ammon.salter@wbs.ac.uk.

Requesting a commercial licence

Email ammon.salter@wbs.ac.uk with the intended use, audience and scale. Licences are usually simple: a scope (the programme, the audience, the period), a fee proportionate to the use, and the same attribution as everyone else. Commercial licences are handled by the University of Warwick.
