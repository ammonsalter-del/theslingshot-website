For Educators

The Slingshot is designed for educators teaching entrepreneurial strategy in nascent industries, technology venturing, innovation management, technology commercialisation and university-industry interaction. It builds on the attention-based view of the firm (Ocasio, 1997): attention is the scarce resource, and the game's central tension is allocating it between competing goals, building internal capability while engaging the outside world that holds the resources a venture needs. The simulation provides students with hands-on experience navigating the challenges of building an AI startup, from founding through scaling.

Free, open-source, and grounded in real startup experiences, The Slingshot can be integrated into courses at undergraduate, postgraduate, and executive education levels.

Learning Objectives

🎯 Strategic Decision-Making

Students learn to make resource allocation decisions under uncertainty and time pressure. They balance competing priorities across product development, customer acquisition, operations, and team building while managing limited cash runway.

💰 Entrepreneurial Finance

Students experience venture funding dynamics firsthand: evaluating funding options, understanding dilution and valuation, negotiating with investors, and choosing between bootstrapping and venture capital paths.

🚀 Technology Commercialization

Students navigate the specific challenges of commercializing AI technology: managing technical risk, achieving product-market fit in fast-moving sectors, and competing in markets where technology capabilities evolve rapidly.

⚖️ Trade-offs and Constraints

Students grapple with fundamental entrepreneurial trade-offs: growth vs. profitability, product quality vs. speed to market, equity vs. control, and short-term survival vs. long-term positioning.

The simplest way to use it

The Slingshot needs no licence, no accounts and no timetable slot. It is free, runs in the browser, and was built to be engaging enough that students play it unprompted. The lightest way to adopt it is a link in your course page or group chat. Students play in their own time, and many replay it chasing a better exit.

Tournaments, class reports and debriefs are all there when you want them.

Using it in class

Early adopters use The Slingshot in many ways, and most cost nothing to set up, across undergraduate, postgraduate and executive courses:

  • Add a link on the course page. Zero adoption cost: nothing to install, no accounts and no fee, with students playing in the browser in their own time.
  • In place of a reading: an experiential alternative to a case or textbook chapter, picked up in the next class.
  • A single 90-minute class with a group debrief comparing strategies and outcomes.
  • A common starting point: give everyone the same opening, such as the same sector and funding choice, then compare and contrast the different paths students take.
  • An assessed individual reflection on the choices students made and why.
  • Team play: small teams play together, arguing through decisions, solving problems and learning from one another, often with plenty of laughter; they can also compete through Trajectory.
  • Alongside a sector mini-case: play the sector, then read how a real company in it (Darktrace, Sylvera, Robin AI, Multiverse, Synthesia, Lendable, Wayve or Isomorphic Labs) navigated the same terrain.
  • Multiple plays: replay with different sectors or strategies and compare outcomes, drawing out how context shapes strategy.

Students also relish the bargaining the game involves: haggling with staff over wages, with funders over term sheets, and with one another, so negotiation and trade-offs run right through the experience.

The Trajectory Wizard

The Trajectory Wizard turns a class's end-of-game reports into a slide deck of class results. Pool the PDFs, attach the Wizard's instruction file to an AI assistant, and the deck comes back: ventures chosen, quarters survived, funding raised, pivots, a top-three podium, and draft questions for the debrief.

Every number must trace back to the reports, and a thirty-second check confirms nothing was invented. The deck is plain on purpose, so you can restyle it. It is part of the educator pack.

  • • No installation required
  • • No student accounts needed

⏱️ Time Commitment

  • • 25-40 minutes to complete one game
  • • 10-15 minutes for brief debrief

Accessibility

The Slingshot is developed against WCAG 2.2 AA. The whole game can be played by keyboard. Screen readers get proper labels, announcements and a sensible focus order, and text contrast meets the standard. The work took eight audit sweeps and a human tester. More detail on what we did is in the GitHub repository.

If a student hits a barrier, email ammon.salter@wbs.ac.uk and we will fix it.

Social & Collaborative Learning

The Slingshot is designed to spark conversation, debate, and shared learning. While students can play individually, the richest learning often emerges when students engage socially with the simulation.

👥 Team Play & Discussion

Students can play in pairs or small groups (2-4 people), making decisions together. This creates natural opportunities for debate about strategy, risk tolerance, and priorities. Groups often discover they have different intuitions about what matters most, and these differences drive learning.

After playing, teams can present their strategies and outcomes to the class, explaining their choices and what they learned. Comparing different approaches reveals there's no single "right" path to success.

🎮 Interactive Mini-Games

The simulation includes three interactive mini-games that create memorable, hands-on experiences with key business activities:

  • 🎪 Conference: Work your booth at an industry conference. Click on delegates walking by, use swag and coffee to attract attention, run demos for serious prospects. Learn about budget management and lead generation under time pressure.
  • 🎤 Investor Pitch: Deliver a 30-second pitch to venture capitalists. Keep the attention meter high, time your demo for maximum impact, read investor reactions. Experience the intensity of pitching for funding.
  • 🚀 Demo Day: You have 60 seconds as visitors approach your booth. Engage leads to see their value, match offers to preferences, choose wisely between quick interactions and expensive demos. Practice prioritizing high-value opportunities.

These mini-games make abstract concepts tangible and create memorable decision moments that students can reflect on during debrief sessions.

💬 Sharing Experiences

Create a shared class experience by having students play the same sectors or compare outcomes across different sectors. Students learn as much from seeing what happened to others as from their own journey. The personalized end-game reports provide concrete starting points for these discussions.

🏆 Friendly Competition

While not the primary goal, light competition can boost engagement. Create leaderboards by valuation, revenue, or other metrics. Students enjoy comparing results and understanding why different strategies led to different outcomes.

Research Foundation

The Slingshot was developed as part of an ESRC-funded research project (ES/X003949/1) exploring entrepreneurship education and innovation management. The simulation is grounded in:

  • • Analysis of 590+ real startup events and decision points
  • • Research on AI sector dynamics and competitive patterns
  • • Studies of venture funding processes and term sheet structures
  • • Empirical findings on early-stage team formation and dynamics

Created by Ammon Salter (Warwick Business School, University of Warwick), with Stefano Baruffaldi (Politecnico di Milano) and Federico Bignone (Warwick Business School).
