For Educators

The Slingshot is designed for educators teaching entrepreneurship, innovation management, strategy, and technology commercialization. The simulation provides students with hands-on experience navigating the challenges of building an AI startup, from founding through scaling.

Free, open-source, and grounded in real startup experiences, The Slingshot can be integrated into courses at undergraduate, postgraduate, and executive education levels.

Learning Objectives

🎯 Strategic Decision-Making

Students learn to make resource allocation decisions under uncertainty and time pressure. They balance competing priorities across product development, customer acquisition, operations, and team building while managing limited cash runway.

💰 Entrepreneurial Finance

Students experience venture funding dynamics firsthand: evaluating funding options, understanding dilution and valuation, negotiating with investors, and choosing between bootstrapping and venture capital paths.

🚀 Technology Commercialization

Students navigate the specific challenges of commercializing AI technology: managing technical risk, achieving product-market fit in fast-moving sectors, and competing in markets where technology capabilities evolve rapidly.

⚖️ Trade-offs and Constraints

Students grapple with fundamental entrepreneurial trade-offs: growth vs. profitability, product quality vs. speed to market, equity vs. control, and short-term survival vs. long-term positioning.

Concepts Covered

The simulation exposes students to 20 core startup and innovation concepts. Click any concept to learn more.

Cash Management & Runway
How much cash you have determines how long your startup survives. Runway is the number of quarters you can operate before running out of money—managing it is the most fundamental skill in entrepreneurship.
Burn Rate
The rate at which your startup spends cash each quarter. Balancing burn rate against growth ambitions is a constant tension—spend too little and you fall behind competitors, spend too much and you run out of runway.
Valuation (Pre-Money vs. Post-Money)
Valuation determines what your company is worth and how much equity you give away when raising capital. Pre-money valuation is your company's value before investment; post-money includes the new capital. Higher valuations mean less dilution for founders.
Equity & Dilution
Every funding round and equity grant dilutes existing shareholders. Students learn how co-founder splits, investor rounds, and employee option pools interact to shape ownership over time—and why protecting equity matters.
Cap Table
The capitalisation table tracks who owns what percentage of your company. The simulation maintains a live cap table that evolves with each funding round, hire, and equity decision, showing students how ownership structures develop.
Bootstrapping vs. Venture Capital
Should you self-fund and grow organically, or raise venture capital to scale quickly? Each path has trade-offs around control, growth speed, risk, and the type of company you can build.
Product-Market Fit
The degree to which your product satisfies real market demand. Achieving product-market fit is the single most important milestone for an early-stage startup—without it, growth spending is wasted.
Strategic Pivots
When your current strategy isn't working, pivoting means fundamentally changing direction—your target market, product, or business model. The simulation forces students to recognise when persistence becomes stubbornness.
Resource Allocation & Opportunity Cost
With limited cash each quarter, every pound spent on R&D is a pound not spent on marketing or hiring. Students learn that resource allocation is about trade-offs, and that the cost of any choice includes the alternatives forgone.
Competitive Positioning
Where you stand relative to competitors determines pricing power, customer acquisition, and investor interest. Students navigate markets with AI rivals, incumbents, and new entrants, learning to differentiate and defend their position.
Team Building & Hiring
Hiring the right people at the right time is critical. Students recruit co-founders and department heads, learning how team composition affects product development, sales capability, and organisational capacity.
Salary vs. Equity Trade-offs
When hiring, you can offer higher salary (preserving equity but increasing burn rate) or more equity (conserving cash but diluting ownership). This trade-off shapes both your finances and your team's incentive alignment.
Team Morale & Culture
A motivated team performs better and stays longer. Decisions around workload, compensation, and company direction all affect morale—and low morale can trigger departures, reduced productivity, and slower progress.
Revenue & ARR
Annual recurring revenue (ARR) is the key metric investors use to gauge traction in software businesses. Growing revenue signals product-market fit and reduces dependence on external funding.
Business Models
How you monetise your AI product—B2B SaaS subscriptions, enterprise licensing, consumer freemium, or platform models. Choosing and adapting your business model affects revenue potential, sales cycles, and scalability.
Exit Strategies
Acquisition, IPO, or sustainable independence—every startup eventually faces the question of its endgame. Exit strategy shapes fundraising decisions, growth targets, and how you position the company to potential acquirers or public markets.
Founder-Market Fit
The alignment between a founder's background and the market they're entering. Students choose from different founder profiles—technical, commercial, or domain expert—each bringing advantages and blind spots to the venture.
Research Commercialisation
Turning cutting-edge AI research into viable products. Students experience the challenges of bridging the gap between academic innovation and market-ready solutions—including technology readiness, user needs, and go-to-market timing.
R&D Investment & Science Strategy
How much to invest in research and development, and when. Students decide whether to pursue incremental improvements or breakthrough capabilities, balancing the long-term payoff of R&D against immediate commercial pressures.
Innovation Grants & Non-Dilutive Funding
Government and institutional grants provide funding without giving up equity. Students weigh the benefits of non-dilutive capital against the effort required to apply and the constraints that often come with grant funding.

Implementation Approaches

The Slingshot is flexible enough to support multiple pedagogical approaches:

In-Class Activity (60-90 minutes)

Students play individually or in small groups during class time. Follow with structured debrief comparing strategies and outcomes.

Best for: Illustrating specific concepts like resource allocation or funding decisions

Assignment (Out of Class)

Students play on their own time and submit reflections on their decisions, strategies, and outcomes.

Best for: Deeper engagement with strategy formulation and reflection on entrepreneurial decision-making

Team Competition

Teams compete to achieve the best outcomes. Create leaderboards and have teams present their strategies and results.

Best for: Generating energy and engagement, encouraging strategic thinking and peer learning

Case Study Complement

Use alongside traditional case studies. Students play the simulation then discuss real startup cases, comparing their choices to actual entrepreneur decisions.

Best for: Connecting experiential learning with real-world analysis

Multiple Rounds

Students play multiple times with different sectors or strategies, then compare outcomes and reflect on what they learned.

Best for: Exploring how context shapes strategy and understanding path dependencies

Social & Collaborative Learning

The Slingshot is designed to spark conversation, debate, and shared learning. While students can play individually, the richest learning often emerges when students engage socially with the simulation.

👥 Team Play & Discussion

Students can play in pairs or small groups (2-4 people), making decisions together. This creates natural opportunities for debate about strategy, risk tolerance, and priorities. Groups often discover they have different intuitions about what matters most—and these differences drive learning.

After playing, teams can present their strategies and outcomes to the class, explaining their choices and what they learned. Comparing different approaches reveals there's no single "right" path to success.

🎮 Interactive Mini-Games

The simulation includes three interactive mini-games that create memorable, hands-on experiences with key business activities:

  • 🎪 Conference: Work your booth at an industry conference. Click on delegates walking by, use swag and coffee to attract attention, run demos for serious prospects. Learn about budget management and lead generation under time pressure.
  • 🎤 Investor Pitch: Deliver a 30-second pitch to venture capitalists. Keep the attention meter high, time your demo for maximum impact, read investor reactions. Experience the intensity of pitching for funding.
  • 🚀 Demo Day: You have 60 seconds as visitors approach your booth. Engage leads to see their value, match offers to preferences, choose wisely between quick interactions and expensive demos. Practice prioritizing high-value opportunities.

These mini-games make abstract concepts tangible and create memorable decision moments that students can reflect on during debrief sessions.

💬 Sharing Experiences

Create a shared class experience by having students play the same sectors or compare outcomes across different sectors. Students learn as much from seeing what happened to others as from their own journey. The personalized end-game reports provide concrete starting points for these discussions.

🏆 Friendly Competition

While not the primary goal, light competition can boost engagement. Create leaderboards by valuation, revenue, or other metrics. Students enjoy comparing results and understanding why different strategies led to different outcomes.

Research Foundation

The Slingshot was developed as part of an ESRC-funded research project (ES/X003949/1) exploring entrepreneurship education and innovation management. The simulation is grounded in:

  • • Analysis of 550+ real startup events and decision points
  • • Research on AI sector dynamics and competitive patterns
  • • Studies of venture funding processes and term sheet structures
  • • Empirical findings on early-stage team formation and dynamics

Developed by Ammon Salter (University of Warwick), Stefano Baruffaldi (Politecnico di Milano), and Federico Bignone (Politecnico di Milano).
